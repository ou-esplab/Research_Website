Research

Earth System Prediction on Subseasonal to Seasonal Timescales

Overview

The ESP Lab focuses on understanding the sources of predictability and the limitations of current prediction systems on subseasonal to seasonal (S2S) timescales — the critical "gap" between medium-range weather forecasting (up to ~2 weeks) and seasonal climate outlooks.

Our research spans model diagnostics, multi-model ensemble design, coupled earth system processes, and operational forecast evaluation.

Research Areas

🌡️ S2S Prediction

Subseasonal to Seasonal (S2S) Prediction

Investigating what physical processes drive predictability at 2-week to seasonal lead times, including the Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO), stratosphere–troposphere coupling, and soil moisture memory.

📈 NMME

Multi-Model Ensembles (NMME)

Development and evaluation of the North American Multi-Model Ensemble (NMME) system, including bias correction, calibration, and probabilistic forecast skill assessment.

🌊 Coupled Processes

Coupled Earth System Processes

Studying atmosphere–ocean–land–sea ice interactions that provide predictability, including ENSO teleconnections and land surface feedbacks.

🔍 Model Diagnostics

Model Evaluation & Diagnostics

Identifying and characterizing systematic biases in coupled climate models that degrade forecast skill, and developing strategies to mitigate their impact.

💧 Applications

Forecast Applications

Translating S2S prediction advances into actionable information for water resources management, agriculture, energy planning, and disaster preparedness.
